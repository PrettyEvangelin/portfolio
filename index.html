How I got here

A short story, in four chapters.

1

It started with questions

During my Computer Science degree at PES Institute of Technology and Management, what I enjoyed most was making data answer something. SQL and Python were the first tools that let me do it.

2

Then I wanted it to predict

I moved from reading data to learning from it: scikit-learn, XGBoost and TensorFlow. That led to a multimodal lie-detection system that reads voice, face and text together, and a paper in IJSREM.

3

Then I wanted people to use it

A model nobody can reach isn’t much use. So I learned FastAPI, Docker and AWS, and as an intern I turned raw business data into Power BI dashboards that teams could act on.

4

Now it talks back

My latest build lets non-technical people ask a campaign database questions in plain English, using RAG. That’s the space I want to keep exploring.

I’m looking for work where Python meets AI, ML or GenAI, and I’m happy to learn the rest on the way.

Fintech and compliance

Compliance Orchestration Prototype

A small backend that screens a name against sanctions and company-registry data and returns one unified risk view.

PythonREST APIOpenSanctionsCompanies HouseRender
GitHub repository
  • Built a POST /screen endpoint that checks a name against OpenSanctions (sanctions and PEP data) and the UK Companies House registry in one request.
  • Wrote a normalizer that maps two very differently shaped vendor responses into one schema: source, entity name, risk score and flags.
  • Scores risk 1 for sanctions targets, high-risk topics such as debarment or export control, and companies that aren’t active. Overall risk is the maximum across matches.
  • Deployed on Render. Next steps: fuzzy name matching, graded scoring, case management, caching and authentication.
